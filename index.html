PART 02 · LEARNING HIGHLIGHTS

Understanding art,
one idea at a time.

Five concepts that help explain how visual choices can communicate emotion, meaning, and cultural experience.

01

Elements of Art

Line, shape, form, color, value, texture, and space are the basic visual building blocks of an artwork. Artists combine them to guide attention and create mood.

Why it matters: Recognizing the elements helps me explain what I see instead of only saying whether I like a piece.

02

Principles of Art

Balance, contrast, emphasis, movement, rhythm, pattern, unity, and proportion describe how elements are organized. These choices influence how an artwork feels and how viewers look at it.

Why it matters: The principles help me understand why a composition feels calm, energetic, balanced, or dramatic.

03

Nature and Functions of Art

Art can express emotion, preserve culture, communicate ideas, document events, question society, or serve a practical purpose. Its meaning may change depending on the viewer and context.

Why it matters: Art is more than decoration; it can connect personal experiences with wider social and cultural stories.

04

Artistic Media & Techniques

Artists choose materials and methods—such as oil paint, watercolor, printmaking, photography, or digital tools—to achieve particular effects. Brushwork and surface can also affect mood.

Why it matters: Knowing the medium helps me appreciate the skill, decisions, and limits involved in making an artwork.

05

Art Across Cultures

Western and Asian traditions contain many styles shaped by different histories, beliefs, environments, and communities. No single tradition represents all artists within a region.

Why it matters: Comparing works encourages respect for different ways of expressing identity, spirituality, beauty, and everyday life.

RENAISSANCE · ITALY01

Mona Lisa

Leonardo da Vinci · c. 1503–1519 · Oil on poplar

Soft transitions between light and shadow, balanced composition, and the subject’s ambiguous expression create a quiet sense of mystery. The landscape behind her adds depth without taking attention away from her face.

My appreciation I find this painting interesting because it does not reveal every emotion directly. The slight smile invites viewers to form their own interpretations. I also appreciate how the artist uses subtle color changes and gradual shading rather than strong outlines. These techniques make the face appear lifelike and help create a calm atmosphere. The painting reminds me that emotion in art does not always need bright colors or dramatic movement. Sometimes a small expression, careful observation, and a balanced composition can make an image memorable. It also shows how artistic skill and mystery can work together across centuries.

POST-IMPRESSIONISM · NETHERLANDS02

The Starry Night

Vincent van Gogh · 1889 · Oil on canvas

Swirling brushstrokes create movement across a blue night sky, while yellow stars and the moon provide vivid contrast. The dark cypress tree connects the foreground with the sky.

My appreciation I chose this artwork because its colors and energetic lines make the night feel alive. The blue tones can suggest quietness or loneliness, while the bright yellow stars bring warmth and hope. I like that the painting does not present the landscape as a perfectly realistic view. Instead, the visible brushstrokes communicate a personal response to the world. The curved forms guide my eyes around the composition, making the sky seem to move. This work helped me understand that artists can transform ordinary scenery into an emotional experience. It also reminds me that different viewers may feel different things when looking at the same painting.

IMPRESSIONISM · FRANCE03

Impression, Sunrise

Claude Monet · 1872 · Oil on canvas

Loose brushwork and a limited palette capture the changing atmosphere of a harbor. The orange sun stands out against cool blue-gray tones, creating contrast and a clear focal point.

My appreciation I appreciate this painting because it focuses on a moment and its atmosphere rather than carefully outlining every object. The orange sun immediately attracts my attention, while the cool colors around it make the scene feel misty and quiet. The loose marks suggest that light and weather are always changing. It teaches me that an artwork can communicate an impression instead of recording every detail. I also find it interesting that a simple contrast between warm and cool colors can guide the viewer through the scene. This painting encourages me to notice how light, color, and mood affect the way I experience everyday places.

JAPANESE ART · EDO PERIOD04

The Great Wave off Kanagawa

Katsushika Hokusai · c. 1831 · Woodblock print

The enormous curling wave contrasts with distant Mount Fuji. Strong outlines, repeated curves, and shades of blue create movement and tension while the small boats emphasize the scale of nature.

My appreciation I find this print powerful because it makes nature appear both beautiful and overwhelming. The wave’s curved shape creates movement, and its size makes the boats look fragile. Mount Fuji appears small in the distance, yet its stable shape balances the energetic foreground. The limited blue-and-white palette unifies the image while keeping the wave visually strong. I also appreciate that the work comes from Japanese woodblock-printing traditions, where line, pattern, and careful design are essential. It reminds me that art can show the relationship between people and nature without using words. The scene makes me feel awe and respect for forces larger than myself.

INDIAN ART · ANCIENT PERIOD05

Bodhisattva Padmapani

Artist unknown · Ajanta Caves, India · c. 5th century · Mural painting

The figure’s graceful posture, gentle expression, jewelry, and lotus create a sense of compassion and spiritual dignity. Earthy pigments and carefully modeled forms reflect the mural tradition of the Ajanta caves.

My appreciation This mural interests me because the figure communicates calmness through posture and expression. The lowered gaze and the lotus create a contemplative mood, while the ornaments and flowing forms add visual richness. I appreciate that the work belongs to a Buddhist cave complex, where art supported religious reflection and storytelling. Its historical setting helps me understand that artworks are connected to the beliefs and practices of the communities that made and viewed them. The subdued colors also show that an artwork can be expressive without using a bright palette. For me, the mural suggests compassion, patience, and the importance of looking beyond outward appearance.

PHILIPPINE ART · 20TH CENTURY06

Planting Rice

Fernando Amorsolo · commonly dated 1920s · Painting

Warm sunlight, green fields, and figures working together present rural life through an idealized pastoral scene. The arrangement draws attention to labor, community, and the agricultural landscape.

My appreciation I connect with this painting because farming and rice are important parts of Filipino life and history. The warm light makes the scene welcoming, while the repeated figures create rhythm across the field. I notice how the landscape and people are presented together, showing a relationship between community and the land. The image can encourage pride in Filipino culture, although I also recognize that an idealized scene may not show every difficulty experienced by farmers. This makes me think about how art can celebrate a culture while also shaping the way people imagine it. I appreciate the painting as a starting point for discussing work, tradition, and national identity.

The Starry Night.

A personal analysis of how color, movement, and expressive brushwork create an emotional landscape.

POST-IMPRESSIONISM1889

Vincent van Gogh

The Starry Night · Oil on canvas · Museum of Modern Art, New York

Subject and historical background

The painting presents a night sky filled with swirling forms, glowing stars, and a crescent moon above a quiet village. A dark cypress tree rises in the foreground. Van Gogh painted it in 1889 while staying at the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum in Saint-Rémy, France. The work is not simply a neutral record of a view; its expressive forms transform the landscape into a personal artistic interpretation.

Elements and principles

Blue dominates the sky and creates a strong field of color, while yellow and white stars provide contrast and emphasis. Curved lines and repeated brushstrokes establish rhythm and movement. The dark cypress gives the composition vertical balance, and the village’s smaller, calmer shapes contrast with the active sky. The difference between cool and warm colors strengthens the painting’s emotional impact.

Meaning and interpretation

The painting can suggest wonder, isolation, hope, or the powerful energy of nature. These are interpretations rather than a single confirmed meaning. Its emotional effect comes from the way color and movement work together, allowing viewers to bring their own experiences to the image.

My personal appreciation

I selected The Starry Night because it helped me see that art can communicate emotion without telling a complete story. The sky seems to move through its curved lines and thick brushstrokes, while the small village below feels quiet and still. This contrast makes the painting feel both energetic and peaceful. The deep blues create a reflective mood, and the yellow stars stand out like small points of warmth. I also admire how Van Gogh changes the appearance of the landscape to express a personal vision instead of copying every detail realistically.

Before studying art appreciation, I might have focused mainly on whether a painting looked realistic or attractive. Now I can look more carefully at its elements and principles and ask why the artist made particular choices. The contrast, rhythm, emphasis, and balance in this work all contribute to its effect. I understand that viewers may interpret the painting differently depending on their experiences. For me, it represents the idea that uncertainty and hope can exist together. It reminds me that art can make an ordinary subject—a night sky—feel personal, memorable, and full of meaning.

PART 06 · FINAL REFLECTION

Art changed the way
I look at the world.

Throughout this semester, my understanding of art has changed. Before taking Art Appreciation, I mostly thought of art as drawing, painting, or making something beautiful. I now understand that art can also communicate emotions, preserve culture, record history, and express ideas about society. An artwork can have meaning beyond what is immediately visible, and learning about its context helps me understand it more deeply.

The topic that influenced me most is the use of color to express emotion. Studying The Starry Night showed me that artists do not always need realistic details to communicate a feeling. Color contrast, repeated lines, movement, and composition can shape the way viewers respond. This encouraged me to look at images more carefully and consider how artists make their decisions.

Exploring Western and Asian art also broadened my perspective. The artworks come from different periods and cultural settings, so their styles and meanings are not all the same. I learned to consider the traditions, beliefs, and historical circumstances behind each work instead of judging it only by my personal taste. Philippine art also reminds me that artworks can represent local experiences, values, and identity.

I realized that art, culture, history, and society influence one another. Artists are shaped by the world around them, and their works can influence how later generations remember and understand that world. As a future IT professional, I can apply these lessons to web design by thinking carefully about color, visual hierarchy, balance, accessibility, and the feelings a digital experience creates. Art Appreciation taught me to be more observant, open-minded, and respectful of different forms of expression.
